Comfy

Making credit purchases clear and calm for Ukraine’s largest retailer of home appliances and electronics.

Role
UX / Product Designer
Team
4 designers
Year
2024
Business goal
Credit order share 30% → 40%

Problem

Buying on credit felt risky. Terms were unclear, nobody explained how the final amount was calculated, and checkout overloaded people at the moment they had to commit.

What we did

15 interviews, a survey of 178 shoppers and a review of 54 competitor features led to a clearer credit block, a bank-offer drawer with a payment calculator, a calmer checkout and video consultations.

Result

In moderated tests the redesign reached 100% task completion with zero errors, against 75% for the current flow. In Maze, the success rate rose from 87.5% to 92.3% between iterations.

Discover

Desk research, a kick-off with the business, competitors, 15 interviews and a survey.

COMFY is the largest retailer of home appliances and electronics in Ukraine. It has run an omnichannel business for over 10 years and is the #2 player in Ukrainian e-commerce.

Desk research

Before the kick-off meeting, we explored the context from several angles to prepare informed questions and spot early opportunities:

  • Analyzed traffic data and user behavior using SimilarWeb.
  • Reviewed customer reviews to find recurring complaints and concerns.
  • Studied Comfy’s credit purchase flow against competitors to spot UX patterns.

What the business told us

  • Not every product or service is eligible for credit; it depends on vendor agreements.
  • Adding services to a purchase, such as setup or warranty, can unlock better terms: 12 months instead of 7.
  • The company works with several banks to offer installments.
  • People choose credit for different reasons: some lack the funds, others have the money but prefer to spread the cost.

After the kick-off we combined a Business Model Canvas and a Lean Canvas, adapted to cover every aspect of the business. It helped us understand the domain and organise everything we had collected.

Competitor analysis

We reviewed UX flows, UI patterns and best practices, and sorted features into Points of Parity, which every store in the domain needs, and Points of Difference, which can set a product apart.

3direct competitors: MOYO, Stylus, Foxtrot
3secondary: ROZETKA, ALLO, Citrus
54features compared

What needs to be better

  • Credit terms and payment conditions were often unclear or overly complex.
  • Users struggled to understand how the final amount was calculated.
  • There was no guidance on choosing the most favourable bank offer.

15 in-depth interviews

We looked for what makes people buy, or not buy, on credit: their barriers, motivations, triggers and context.

“I didn’t read the terms, like everyone who doesn’t read the small print. All I did was multiply the monthly payment by six and compare it with the order price.”

A shopper in an interview, translated from Ukrainian

Follow-up survey

To check interview insights at scale, we turned recurring themes into survey questions. For a 95% confidence level we needed 385 respondents.

178 / 385responses against the target sample

The survey tested four insights: clarity of information, package deals, comparing credit terms, and wanting to see a product in person before buying.

Define

Jobs to be done, the customer journey and the value proposition.

Comfy is a large product, and narrowing it to one or two personas wasn’t realistic. We used Jobs to be Done instead. The JTBD timeline showed the real-life context, decision triggers and emotional journey from the first need to the final purchase.

The customer journey map showed pain points, motivations and friction at each step of the purchase, so we could improve where it mattered most.

The value proposition canvas showed which needs Comfy already meets, and which barriers create friction or distrust.

Comfy helps people buy conveniently and right away by offering credit and installments through many banks, without visiting a bank or a store.

What Comfy covers well

  • Inspecting the product in store
  • Flexible credit through familiar banks
  • No interest on installments
  • Splitting large payments over time
  • Bonuses and loyalty rewards
  • A quick credit-based purchase

What it doesn’t fully cover

  • Effortful or uncomfortable contact with banks
  • Website and app that feel inconvenient
  • Anxiety about repayment and credit reliability
  • Uncertainty about returns on credit purchases
  • Confusing or non-transparent terms
  • Not knowing if promotions apply to installments

Develop

Three problems worth solving, How Might We, and a MoSCoW-ranked MVP.

From the research we picked the problems with the biggest impact on trust, decisions and completion. Each is backed by direct quotes from interviews.

These became the base for How Might We mapping, which guided ideation.

From ideas to action

We generated many solutions and ranked them with MoSCoW: must-haves first, should-haves if possible, could-haves for the backlog. It aligned the team on a realistic MVP with the highest user and business impact.

Deliver

Solutions for each problem, two rounds of usability testing, and ideas for the business goal.

1. Lack of transparency in credit terms

  • Bank icons weren’t clickable; they only opened a tooltip.
  • The tooltip held generic information that didn’t help anyone choose.
  • “Buy on credit” and “Buy” led to the same drawer, with no difference in the flow.

We put a visible “Оплата частинами” (Pay in installments) button next to the main “Купити” (Buy) button, showing the lowest monthly payment the banks offer.

We renamed it from “Купити в кредит” (Buy on credit) after interviews showed that the word “credit” made people anxious.

A “Як це працює” (How it works) button inside the calculator opens the full terms with a timeline of payments.

2. Cognitive overload

  • Product details, total cost and credit info disappeared while scrolling.
  • Completed form sections looked the same as empty ones.
  • The product and payment summary stays visible while scrolling.
  • Completed blocks change colour, so progress is easy to scan.
  • The store chosen on the product page carries over to checkout.

People also skimmed delivery options and struggled to spot stores with stock. We highlighted “Pick up today” in green and kept the other options neutral.

3. Uncertainty about product suitability

People wanted to see a product in person before committing to credit. We added a video consultation on the product page.

Ideas for the business goal

To grow the share of credit orders, we prepared four ideas for the product and marketing teams.

Outcome

A credit flow that is clear, calm and tested.

The project uncovered real pain points and turned the credit purchase into something transparent, intuitive and emotionally comfortable, because “credit” shouldn’t feel like a horror story.

Testing showed the redesign working: in the second moderated round every participant completed the task, with no errors and less time than in the current flow. The ideas for growing credit orders are ready for the product and marketing teams.
