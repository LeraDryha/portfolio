<Chapter id="discover" title="Discover" sub="Desk research, a kick-off with the business, competitors, 15 interviews and a survey.">

COMFY is the largest retailer of home appliances and electronics in Ukraine. It has run an omnichannel business for over 10 years and is the #2 player in Ukrainian e-commerce.

### Desk research

Before the kick-off meeting, we explored the context from several angles to prepare informed questions and spot early opportunities:

- Analyzed traffic data and user behavior using SimilarWeb.
- Reviewed customer reviews to find recurring complaints and concerns.
- Studied Comfy’s credit purchase flow against competitors to spot UX patterns.

<Figure src="/work/comfy/c03.jpg" width={1600} height={1133} alt="Desk research board: traffic data, customer reviews and the existing credit flow." caption="Desk research board." />

### What the business told us

- Not every product or service is eligible for credit; it depends on vendor agreements.
- Adding services to a purchase, such as setup or warranty, can unlock better terms: 12 months instead of 7.
- The company works with several banks to offer installments.
- People choose credit for different reasons: some lack the funds, others have the money but prefer to spread the cost.

After the kick-off we combined a Business Model Canvas and a Lean Canvas, adapted to cover every aspect of the business. It helped us understand the domain and organise everything we had collected.

<Figure src="/work/comfy/c04.jpg" width={1600} height={938} alt="Combined Business Model Canvas and Lean Canvas for Comfy’s credit purchases." caption="Business Model Canvas and Lean Canvas, combined." />

### What needs to be better

- Credit terms and payment conditions were often unclear or overly complex.
- Users struggled to understand how the final amount was calculated.
- There was no guidance on choosing the most favourable bank offer.

### 15 in-depth interviews

We looked for what makes people buy, or not buy, on credit: their barriers, motivations, triggers and context.

### 1. Lack of transparency in credit terms

- Bank icons weren’t clickable; they only opened a tooltip.
- The tooltip held generic information that didn’t help anyone choose.
- “Buy on credit” and “Buy” led to the same drawer, with no difference in the flow.

<Figure src="/work/comfy/c15.jpg" width={1600} height={1243} alt="Original product page with non-clickable bank icons and identical buttons." label="Before" caption="Bank icons only open a tooltip; both buttons do the same thing." />

We put a visible “Оплата частинами” (Pay in installments) button next to the main “Купити” (Buy) button, showing the lowest monthly payment the banks offer.

<Note>We renamed it from “Купити в кредит” (Buy on credit) after interviews showed that the word “credit” made people anxious.</Note>

<FigurePair>
  <HalfFigure src="/work/comfy/c17.jpg" width={1600} height={1224} alt="Bank offers redesigned as clickable buttons with conditions per bank." label="After" caption="Each bank offer is a button with its own terms." />
  <HalfFigure src="/work/comfy/c18.jpg" width={1600} height={1340} alt="Drawer listing all credit offers with a payment calculator." label="After" caption="All offers in one drawer, with a calculator." />
</FigurePair>

### 2. Cognitive overload

- Product details, total cost and credit info disappeared while scrolling.
- Completed form sections looked the same as empty ones.

- Completed blocks change colour, so progress is easy to scan.

<Chapter id="outcome" title="Outcome" sub="A credit flow that is clear, calm and tested.">

The project uncovered real pain points and turned the credit purchase into something transparent, intuitive and emotionally comfortable, because “credit” shouldn’t feel like a horror story.

Testing showed the redesign working: in the second moderated round every participant completed the task, with no errors and less time than in the current flow. The ideas for growing credit orders are ready for the product and marketing teams.
