Our task manager had an overloaded and fragmented task creation flow. Key actions like adding attachments, notes, or setting a location were hidden in separate tabs, while the form was cluttered with rarely used fields like “Due by” and “Timeframe.”

As a result, many users dropped off midway or skipped important settings altogether.

I led a UX audit, conducted usability tests, and redesigned the entire flow — simplifying the interface, prioritizing essential actions, and reworking the repeat logic to better reflect real user needs.

<Chapter id="broken" n={1} of={4} title="What was broken">

### 1. Low conversion

Only 32.78% of users complete task creation. This indicates friction in the flow — either due to complexity, unclear structure, or distracting UI. It may also point to cognitive overload or a lack of clarity and motivation to finish the action.

<Figure src="/work/task-creation/02.webp" width={1800} height={964} alt="Mixpanel funnel of task creation: 32.78% of users finish." caption="Task creation funnel in Mixpanel." />

### 2. Cognitive overload

The users who provided feedback through support conversations and in-app reports mentioned that the task creation form felt “overloaded” and included “unnecessary fields.” The form displayed every possible input field upfront even those rarely used, which led to friction, confusion, and slower task completion.

### 3. Unused fields

Only 12% of tasks included a value for the ‘timeframe’ or ‘due by’ fields. This was confirmed through Mixpanel event data.

<DeviceRow shots={[{ src: "/work/task-creation/old-labels.webp", width: 640, height: 1313, alt: "The old form: a long column of fields, from Status to Repeat, all shown at once.", caption: "Every field upfront" }]} />

### 4. Disjointed layout

Additional features, such as adding notes or attaching files, were placed in separate tabs, causing users who provided feedback to be unaware of their availability. Fields like notes, attachments, or location were scattered across different sections, making it difficult for users to quickly find and access these options, which are also important to them.

### Key insights

- Users want to quickly specify the title, date, reminder, and, if needed, repetition.
- Additional fields (location, notes, attachments) should be accessible but don’t need to be immediately visible.
- People expect repetition to work like in Google Calendar: “on the 4th day of every month until the end of December.”

</Chapter>

<Chapter id="changes" n={3} of={4} title="What I changed">

### One screen, logical structure

All core functions were grouped on a single screen with clearly separated blocks. Secondary fields are hidden by default but still easily accessible.

### Smart default for date

Task date is pre-filled as “Today” to reduce effort. Advanced settings (e.g. presets are added for quick selection, enabling users to set the desired date and specific time with ease) are collapsed and available on demand.

<Figure src="/work/task-creation/07.webp" width={1800} height={1280} alt="The new task creation screen with the date pre-filled as Today." caption="One screen, the date pre-filled as Today." />

### Fully flexible repeat

After analyzing competitors and the most popular tools users rely on, all desired options were incorporated to provide greater flexibility and freedom for the user.

The repeat component was redesigned to support complex schedules (e.g., “every Tue and Fri,” “on day 2,” or “on the first Wednesday every 1 month”) and end conditions like “until October 24” or “after 30 occurrences.”

Additionally, the copy was revised for clarity — instead of a vague “Frequency - 1 month,” it now says “Repeat every 1 month,” and instead of “End - May 2, 2025,” it now clearly states “Repeat ends – May 2, 2025.”
